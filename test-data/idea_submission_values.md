## Step 1 — Describe

**Select:** An idea

**Your idea in your own words:**

> I propose a secure document-completeness check for small-business account onboarding. Before a customer submits an application, the bank would show a checklist of the documents required for that business type and let the customer upload them through the bank’s existing authenticated channel. The system would check only whether required document types are present and readable enough for staff review; it would not decide eligibility, interpret documents with an external AI service, or approve an account. If an item is missing, the customer would receive a specific request before the case enters the manual review queue. A small pilot could measure whether this reduces avoidable follow-up and helps customers complete onboarding sooner.

## Step 2 — Develop your idea

**Idea title:**

> Secure document-completeness check for small-business onboarding

**Summary:**

> Show small-business applicants a tailored document checklist and identify missing items before manual review, using the bank’s authenticated upload channel and human approval controls.

**Problem statement:**

> Small-business onboarding cases can reach manual review with required documents missing. Staff then need to request additional items, and customers may not know what is outstanding or why the case is delayed. This creates avoidable follow-up and makes completion time less predictable.

**Proposed solution:**

> Pilot a checklist based on the approved document requirements for each small-business application type. Let customers upload through the existing authenticated bank channel. Check document presence and basic file readability only, then show a clear list of missing or unreadable items. Keep eligibility decisions and document interpretation with authorized staff. Record checklist changes and staff actions in the existing audit trail.

**Expected benefits:**

> Customers can see what is still needed and respond before a case waits in a review queue. Staff may spend less time sending routine missing-document requests and more time on substantive review. The pilot can measure first-time completeness, follow-up volume, and time from application to complete-file review.

**Possible risks:**

> A checklist could be inaccurate if requirements change or are configured for the wrong business type. Customers may misunderstand a “complete” checklist as approval. Uploaded documents and checklist status must remain within approved bank systems, with role-based access, retention controls, and audit logging. The pilot should provide a clear correction path, preserve manual review, and never use the checklist to approve or reject an application.

**Differentiation from existing work (if shown):**

> This proposal checks document presence before manual review. It does not assess creditworthiness, interpret document contents with external AI, or make an onboarding decision.

## Step 3 — Questions reviewers may ask

**Who experiences this problem today?**

> Small-business customers completing account onboarding, and the authorized onboarding staff who review their applications and request missing documents.

**What happens today?**

> A customer submits the application and available documents. If required items are missing or unreadable, staff contact the customer, wait for a response, and review the updated file. The customer may not have a clear view of all outstanding items.

**What is needed to make this work?**

> An approved and maintained checklist by application type, integration with the existing authenticated upload channel, clear missing-item messages, role-based staff access, audit events, and a pilot owner from onboarding and compliance.

**What could make the proposal difficult?**

> Keeping checklist rules current across business types and jurisdictions, integrating with existing upload and case-management systems, protecting sensitive documents, and ensuring customers understand that a complete checklist is not an approval.

**How would you know the change worked?**

> Compare pilot cases with a documented baseline. Track first-time complete-file rate, missing-document follow-ups per case, elapsed time to complete-file review, customer abandonment, checklist correction rate, and any privacy or control incidents. Continue only if the pilot improves the agreed measures without weakening controls.

## Step 5 — Impact numbers

The form accepts scores from 0 to 3. Use these high-impact ratings only to exercise the scoring and submission path; replace them with evidence-based ratings for a real proposal.

| Field | Test value | Explanation |
|---|---:|---|
| Turnaround time | `3` | Test assumption: preventing avoidable missing-item follow-up could materially shorten the time to a complete-file review. Measure the baseline and pilot elapsed time before claiming an actual reduction. |
| Volume | `3` | Test assumption: the checklist could apply to multiple small-business onboarding cases. Confirm eligible case volume and pilot coverage before estimating reach. |
| Expected savings or revenue | `3` | Test assumption: less routine follow-up could free staff capacity and reduce repeat handling. Do not report a financial saving until operational and Finance teams validate the calculation. |

**Turnaround explanation:**

> For the test, rate the potential as high. In a real pilot, compare median time from application submission to complete-file review against a pre-pilot baseline, and report the sample size and period.

**Volume explanation:**

> For the test, rate the potential reach as high. In a real pilot, count eligible small-business applications and report what share used the checklist successfully.

**Value explanation:**

> For the test, rate the potential operational value as high. In a real pilot, estimate staff time spent on missing-document follow-up using an agreed method, then have Finance validate any monetary conversion.
